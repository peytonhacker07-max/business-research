Homeownership brings comfort and pride — but it also brings maintenance responsibilities and unexpected repair costs. Many homeowners look to home warranty companies for peace of mind, hoping for protection from surprise expenses. Unfortunately, not all home warranty companies deliver on their promises.

In recent years, more reports have surfaced of homeowners struggling to get covered repairs completed, vendors not being paid, and contractors refusing warranty work altogether. This leaves many wondering: how do I know if a home warranty company is credible? And is it better to partner locally for preventive maintenance instead?

Let’s break it down 👇

## 🏠 The Truth About Home Warranty Companies  {bold}

A home warranty is a contract designed to cover repair or replacement costs for key home systems and appliances — plumbing, HVAC, water heaters, etc. On paper, that sounds great. The challenge? Many homeowners discover the fine print tells a different story.

Common frustrations include:

- Claim denials for “pre-existing conditions” or “improper installation”

- Long service delays waiting for warranty-approved technicians

- Low payout limits, leaving homeowners with unexpected bills

- Vendors not being paid, resulting in poor service or canceled appointments

- Beware, home warranty solutions offered by Realtors or Mortgage companies don’t always have your best interest in mind, often times these agencies are paid stipend for selling their home warranties to buyers and sellers.

In other words, the protection you thought you had might not be there when you need it most.

## 🔍 How to Evaluate if a Warranty Company Is Credible  {bold}

If you’re still considering a home warranty, take the time to vet the provider carefully. Here’s how:

- Check licensing and rating agencies

- Look for reviews with the Better Business Bureau (BBB) and state consumer protection agencies.

- Read both positive and negative feedback carefully to identify patterns of complaint.

- Ask about contractor payment terms

- Call a few local plumbers, HVAC techs, or electricians to see which home warranty companies they’ll work with — and which ones they avoid due to unpaid invoices.

- Read the fine print

- Review claim limitations, exclusions, and what “normal wear and tear” truly covers.

- Watch for vague wording around “authorized service providers.”

- Ask about response time and claim approval process

- How fast are emergencies handled? Is this in writing?

- Can you choose your own contractor, or must you wait for theirs?

- Avoid any company that pressures you to sign up immediately

- Reliable home warranty companies will allow you time to read and compare coverage before committing.
