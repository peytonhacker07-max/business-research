What Is Reverse Osmosis?

Reverse osmosis is a water filtration process that uses a specialized membrane to reduce many dissolved impurities from water. It is commonly used in under-sink drinking water systems to provide filtered water through a dedicated faucet.

A reverse osmosis system may help reduce:

  • Sediment
  • Chlorine taste and odor
  • Dissolved solids
  • Certain minerals
  • Some common contaminants that affect drinking water quality

This makes reverse osmosis a popular choice for homeowners who want cleaner, fresher-tasting water at home.

Benefits of a Reverse Osmosis System

A professionally installed reverse osmosis water filtration system can offer several important advantages.

Better-Tasting Drinking Water

Reverse osmosis can help improve the taste, smell, and clarity of your drinking water, making it more enjoyable for everyday use.

Cleaner Water for Cooking

Filtered water can improve the quality of water used for coffee, tea, soups, pasta, and other food preparation.

Reduced Dissolved Impurities

RO systems are designed to reduce a wide range of dissolved materials that may affect water quality and taste.

Convenient Point-of-Use Filtration

An under-sink reverse osmosis system provides filtered water at a dedicated faucet without needing to treat every water outlet in the home.

Reliable Everyday Performance

With proper installation and maintenance, a reverse osmosis system can provide consistent drinking water filtration for years.

Signs You May Need Reverse Osmosis Services

You may benefit from professional reverse osmosis services if you notice:

  • Poor-tasting tap water
  • Unpleasant odors in drinking water
  • Concerns about dissolved impurities
  • Cloudy or inconsistent drinking water quality
  • A desire for better water for cooking and beverages
  • An older RO system that is no longer performing well
  • The need for filter changes or maintenance
  • Interest in a dedicated drinking water filtration solution

If your goal is cleaner drinking water at a specific faucet, reverse osmosis may be a strong option.
