# Business Basics — Tennessee edition

Not legal or tax advice — this is the practical checklist to get you
operating legitimately. When real money shows up, spend an hour with a
local accountant; it's ~$150 and worth it.

## Getting legal (in rough order)

1. **Start as a sole proprietor** — in TN you can just... start. If you
   operate under a business name (like "Peak AI Solutions" instead of your
   own name), file a DBA/"assumed name" with your county clerk (~$20).
2. **Form an LLC once you have 2–3 paying clients** (~$300 filing fee in
   TN, file online with the TN Secretary of State). It protects your
   personal stuff if a client dispute ever happens and looks more
   professional on invoices. Don't let LLC paperwork delay your first
   sale — revenue first, then LLC.
3. **Business license:** TN counties/cities require a business tax license
   once you pass ~$3,000/yr in gross receipts (standard license at
   $100K+; minimal activity license below that — county clerk, ~$15).
   Check your county clerk's site when you cross $3K.
4. **Taxes:** you're self-employed — set aside **25–30% of everything you
   collect** in a separate savings account, no exceptions. TN has no state
   income tax (nice), but federal self-employment tax applies. If you're
   under 18 or a dependent, your parents' tax situation is affected —
   loop them in.
5. **Separate money:** open a free business checking account (or at
   minimum a separate personal account used only for the business) from
   day one. Mixing money is the #1 rookie mess.

## Getting paid

- **Square or Stripe invoices** — free to set up, ~3% per transaction,
  clients can pay by card or bank. Square also gives you the free
  appointment booking you'll resell to clients.
- Setup fee (or 50%) BEFORE work starts. Monthly fees on auto-pay
  (card on file) — never chase checks.
- Every deal confirmed in writing before work starts. A text thread
  counts. Minimum terms to state:
  - what's included (list it), what's not
  - price + when it's due
  - monthly fee, what it covers, cancel anytime with 30 days notice
  - client owns their domain, content, and accounts
- When you're a few clients in, turn that into a 1-page service agreement
  (an LLM can draft it; have an adult/lawyer sanity-check it once).

## Simple bookkeeping

One spreadsheet, updated weekly: date, client, what, money in, money out.
That's genuinely enough until ~$2K/mo, then get Wave (free) or QuickBooks.

Track your costs per client (domain, phone number, API usage) so you know
real margin — Offer 2 especially, since it has monthly tool costs.

## Positioning notes

- Name the business something boring and trustworthy ("[Town] Web & Phone
  Solutions" energy) or just use your name. "AI" in the name can scare
  older owners; AI is your tool, not your brand.
- Get a Google Business Profile for YOUR business immediately and ask
  every client for a review. You sell Google presence — yours must be
  spotless.
- Your website can be one page. Practice what you preach: reviews, phone,
  what you do, three examples.

## Ethics rules (these protect the business)

- Clients own everything — domains, accounts, content. You're the manager.
- Never fake reviews, never buy reviews, never review your own clients
  from your account. One fake-review scandal ends a reputation business.
- AI assistant hard rules: never let it guess prices, make medical/legal
  claims, or confirm bookings it can't see. "Let me have the owner text
  you" is always the fallback.
- If you can't deliver something, refund it. A $250 refund is cheap
  compared to a 1-star review in a small town.
