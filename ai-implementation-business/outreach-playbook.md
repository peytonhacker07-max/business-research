# Outreach Playbook — working your 100-lead list

Your list (`../tennessee_businesses_no_website.csv`) is sorted gold:
45 barbershops, 28 nail salons, 12 auto repair, 11 restaurants.

## Priority order

1. **Barbershops with a phone number and 4.5+ rating** — easiest sale.
   Appointment-driven, owner is usually the person holding the clippers,
   and they physically cannot answer the phone while working.
2. **Auto repair** — highest ticket per customer ($100s), so "one saved
   call pays for the service" is easy math.
3. **Nail salons** — appointment-driven, but often walk-in heavy; lead
   with booking + reviews.
4. **Restaurants last** — they get pitched constantly and margins are thin.

Before contacting anyone, spend 2 minutes: check their Google listing is
really theirs, note their rating and review count, and confirm no website.
Mentioning specifics ("you've got a 4.8 with 37 reviews") is what separates
you from spam.

## Channel: walk in > call > text. Never email first.

These businesses don't read email. Walk-ins convert best for barbershops
(go at a slow time — mid-morning weekdays, never Friday/Saturday).

---

## The walk-in script (barbershop version)

Bring your phone with the demo site loaded.

> "Hey — you the owner? I'll be 60 seconds. I'm [name], I live here in
> [town]. I build websites and phone tools for local shops. I noticed
> y'all have a 4.8 on Google — but no website, so when people search
> 'barber near me' they're clicking your competitors. I built a sample of
> what your site could look like — can I show you real quick?"

[show demo on phone]

> "I set this up in a week — site, Google page, online booking. $500 one
> time and $49 a month, and I handle everything. Worst case, take my
> number and think about it. What's the best way to reach you?"

**Rules:** talk to the OWNER only. If they're busy, get a name and time to
come back — that's a win, not a rejection. Always leave with their number
or a comeback time.

## The phone script

> "Hey, is this the owner of [business]? My name's [name], I'm local here
> in [town] — this is a quick one, I promise. I help shops like yours get
> a website and stop losing customers to missed calls. I saw you've got
> great reviews on Google but no site. If I texted you a link to a sample
> website I made for a shop like yours, would you take a look?"

Goal of the call is ONLY to get permission to text the demo. Then text it
within 5 minutes, and follow up in 2 days if silent.

## Follow-up texts

- Day 0 (after call/visit): "Great meeting you — here's that sample site:
  [link]. I can have yours live by [day]. — [name]"
- Day 2: "Any thoughts on the site? Happy to change anything you don't like."
- Day 7: "Last nudge, I promise 🙂 I'm taking on 3 shops this month at my
  founding rate. Want me to hold a spot?"
- Then stop. Mark the lead cold, re-touch in 60 days.

## Objection handling

**"I get all my business from word of mouth."**
> "Totally — and your reviews prove it. The website's just word of mouth
> for people who don't know you yet. When someone hears about you and
> Googles you, right now they find nothing."

**"How much? ... That's too much."**
> "How much is one regular customer worth to you a year? [wait] So if this
> brings you two new regulars, it's paid for itself. And I'm cheaper than
> every agency that'll cold-call you — they start at $3,000."

**"I had a website before, it did nothing."**
> "A website alone does nothing — agreed. The bookings come from the
> Google Business Profile + booking link + missed-call texts working
> together. The site's just the home base."

**"Let me think about it."**
> "Of course. What's the part you want to think over — the price or
> whether it'll work?" [answer that one thing, then:] "How about I follow
> up Thursday?"

**"Are you even old enough to do this?" / "You're just a kid."**
> "Yep — which is why I'm hungry, cheap, and I actually answer my phone.
> Here's a site I built: [demo]. Judge the work, not my age."

## Track everything

Make `outreach-tracker.csv` (or a sheet) with: business, contact name,
phone, date contacted, channel, response, next step, next-touch date.
Every lead has a NEXT STEP or it's marked cold. Do 5 touches a day,
every weekday — that's the whole system. Consistency beats talent here.

## The demo trick

Build the demo for a REAL shop from your list before pitching them — a
one-pager with their actual name, photos of their storefront area, their
real reviews pulled in. "I already built this for you" converts insanely
better than "I could build you something." Costs you an afternoon; you
can reuse the template for all 45 barbershops.
