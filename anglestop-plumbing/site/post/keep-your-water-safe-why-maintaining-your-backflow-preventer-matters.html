Keep Your Water Safe: Why Maintaining Your Backflow Preventer Matters

By Jim Nephew · March 28, 2026

Whether you’re a homeowner or a business owner, your plumbing system does more behind the scenes than you might realize. One of the most important — and often overlooked — components is your backflow preventer. This small device plays a big role in protecting your water supply from contamination.

What Is a Backflow Preventer?

A backflow preventer ensures that water flows in only one direction — into your home or business, not back out into the main supply. Without it, changes in water pressure could cause potentially harmful substances to flow backward into drinking water lines.

Your Responsibility

Local regulations require that all backflow prevention devices be tested annually to confirm they’re in good working order. As the property owner, you’re responsible for keeping this device leak-free, properly maintained, and documented with a certified test report from a certified Tennessee tester, such as Angle Stop Plumbing. Neglecting this simple step could lead to water contamination, service interruptions, or even fines from your local water authority.

Why Routine Maintenance Matters

Just like any other plumbing component, backflow preventers can wear out over time — seals crack, valves stick, and debris builds up. Routine maintenance only can be completed by a certified Tennessee tester and testing to keep your system functioning safely and efficiently, avoiding unexpected water disruptions that can affect your family, tenants, or customers.
