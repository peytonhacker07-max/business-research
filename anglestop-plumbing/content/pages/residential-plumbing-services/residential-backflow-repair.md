## What Is Backflow and Why Repair Matters  {bold}

Backflow happens when water flows in the wrong direction through your plumbing system. This reversal can occur because of backsiphonage or backpressure, creating a risk that contaminants from irrigation systems, fire sprinkler lines, boilers, chemicals, or other cross-connections could enter the potable water supply.

A properly functioning backflow preventer is your first line of defense. When that device is damaged, leaking, or failing inspection, prompt repair is critical. Ignoring a faulty backflow assembly can increase contamination risk, lead to failed inspections, and create compliance issues with local water authorities.

## Signs You May Need Backflow Repair  {bold}

You may need to schedule backflow repair services if you notice any of the following:

- Your backflow device failed a required inspection or annual test

- Water is leaking from the backflow preventer

- You notice unusual pressure changes in your plumbing system

- Your irrigation or plumbing system is not operating normally

- The device is corroded, damaged, or visibly worn

- You received a notice to repair or replace the unit

- Your property has had plumbing changes that may affect backflow performance

Even if the issue seems minor, a faulty backflow assembly should be inspected and repaired as soon as possible.
